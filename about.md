I'm Michael Polanczyk, a junior at Leopold High School in Missouri. I'm interested in aerospace and mechanical engineering, and I spend a lot of my time designing things, programming, building parts, or figuring out how something could work better. I like being involved in the whole process, from an idea or sketch to something I can hold, use, and test.

Most of my projects come from something I'm curious about or a problem I run into. Sometimes that means designing a part for our robot. Other times it means building a calculator to understand an equation from class, working on an RC airplane, or planning a workshop because I need more room for my projects.

## School and how I got here

I go to a small rural high school, where there aren't as many classes or technical opportunities as there would be at a larger school. I attend PLTW pre-engineering at Cape CTC, which gives me time to work on engineering concepts and projects beyond what's available at my high school. I also take dual-credit precalculus through Southeast Missouri State University.

I've maintained straight A's and a 4.0 GPA throughout school so far. Doing well in class matters to me, but I also want to understand how the things I'm learning apply outside an assignment. A lot of my independent projects are a way to keep working on a question after class is over.

Going between school, CTC, robotics, and projects has also made me think about how I use my time. I have more ideas than I can work on at once, so part of learning has been deciding what I can realistically finish and what needs more research first.

After graduating high school, I plan to attend **Missouri S&T and dual major in aerospace and mechanical engineering**. I'm also interested in **a business minor**, or another minor that complements my engineering work and helps me contribute more in my career. I haven't settled on the minor yet. Whether that ends up being a better aircraft system, a more efficient mechanism, or a tool that helps someone else do their work, I want to understand the problem well enough to make a useful contribution.

In college, I want to join student design teams and get more experience working with people who have different technical strengths. I'm looking forward to projects where I can help design, build, and test something while learning from the rest of the team.

## Aircraft, flight, and research

I enjoy designing, building, and flying FPV drones, gliders, and RC planes. Flight brings together a lot of what I like: mechanical design, electronics, programming, weight, and the way a small change can affect the whole system.

In eighth grade, I did a science fair project on **how the shape of an airplane wing affects its lift**. I received a Naval Science Award at the Southeast Missouri Regional Science Fair. That project gave me a chance to take an interest in aircraft and investigate a specific question instead of only reading about it.

I'd like to return to that kind of work with the CAD, printing, and programming experience I've gained since then. One idea is a small wind tunnel with interchangeable wing models and a more repeatable way to take measurements. I'm also interested in research opportunities where the goal is to answer a useful question and collect data that other people can learn from.

My Eclipson Vortex is an ongoing RC airplane build. I'm using Eclipson's aircraft design and working through printing, assembly, component choices, and integration myself. The build has had delays, and my goal is to finish it by the end of summer 2027. It has given me experience with the practical work that comes between having a set of plans and getting an aircraft ready to fly.

Two classmates and I are working with our engineering instructor on designs and ideas for the [American Rocketry Challenge](https://github.com/MadeWithoutCaffeine/Engineering---Projects/blob/main/projects/american-rocketry-challenge/README.md). We're still developing ideas, but I want to try competing and learn more about designing and testing rockets as a team. I also want to work toward Level 1 high-power certification as a separate personal goal.

I'm applying to [Non-Trivial](https://github.com/MadeWithoutCaffeine/Engineering---Projects/blob/main/projects/non-trivial-research/README.md). I hope I get accepted, but I haven't chosen a research topic yet. A friend is applying too, and we plan to support whichever of us gets accepted and contribute to the work together.

I'm also considering returning to the [SEMO science fair during my junior and senior years](https://github.com/MadeWithoutCaffeine/Engineering---Projects/blob/main/projects/semo-science-fair-plans/README.md). I might build a research question around Non-Trivial, rocketry, aircraft design, another existing project, or a new idea. I want to choose based on what I can investigate well.

## Robotics and working with a team

I'm part of **FTC Team 3665, the LEOPOLD Gear Heads**. I'm in my third year of official membership, after being involved for about two years before I was old enough to join.

I'm the team's programmer and primary CAD designer, and I handle 3D printing and custom-part production. I also help with assembly and troubleshooting. I use Autodesk Inventor to design parts and assemblies, then work through how those parts fit on the robot and how the code needs to control them.

Working on both the mechanical and programming sides helps me see how connected they are. A mechanism needs to fit, move properly, and work with the rest of the robot. The code has to account for what the hardware actually does, and testing is where those choices come together.

I also teach teammates parts of programming, CAD, and manufacturing. I want to build on that by helping younger students get started, so more people can contribute to the technical work and keep those skills on the team. Being able to make something is useful, but being able to explain it so someone else can work on it matters too.

## CAD, 3D printing, and independent projects

I use Autodesk Inventor to work through designs before making parts. I like being able to revise a model, print a version, check the fit, and change it based on what I find. Some of that work is for robotics, and some is for my own projects.

At home, I use a **Bambu Lab P2S with an AMS 2 Pro**. I donated my previous P1S to my school so other students could learn to design and make their own projects. I want other people to have a chance to try the tools that helped me get interested in this work.

My gear ratio and torque calculator started with something we were learning in Principles of Engineering. I built it in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to try different gear combinations and understand their effect on torque and speed. I kept the interface simple because my main goal was learning the code and the math.

Building my PC was another project that involved a lot of research before buying anything. I compared components, checked compatibility, and worked through how to fit the system into a compact case. I later upgraded from an Intel Arc A770 to an RTX 3080 and kept the original card.

I also entered the MoDOT bridge competition. My bridge weighed **28.65 grams** and supported **13.2 pounds** before a mistake in tensioning two strings brought the test to an end. When I received it back, the frame was still intact apart from the loose strings. That experience gave me a clear reason to spend more time testing small connections before relying on the complete structure.

It's still a design in progress. I want a place where I can keep tools and printing equipment together, leave work set up between sessions, and have room for CAD and programming. A low-electricity filament dryer is another project I'd like to develop for that space.
